## Observations worth carrying into discovery

- Useful customer-facing findings must distinguish observed website content,
  inferred buyer/customer hypotheses, and unverified recommendations.
- A website may have material strengths—such as detailed local project
  evidence—despite generic heuristics suggesting a gap. Assessment output must
  cite page-level evidence and must show strengths as well as opportunities.
- Clear service, location, project, question-answer, and trust content can make
  a business easier for people and retrieval systems to understand. That does
  not prove a ranking effect, a recommendation, or increased revenue.
- Public inspection can review content, site structure, crawl accessibility,
  metadata, and visible structured data. It cannot establish actual organic
  performance, enquiry conversion, Google Business Profile interactions, or
  complete backlink strength without owner-authorised data or a suitable data
  provider.
- A customer-facing report must not claim a fixed ChatGPT/OpenAI rank. A dated
  sample can instead state whether a business was mentioned, how it was
  described, what sources were present, and what could not be determined.
- Any future use of third-party SEO/backlink data must state its source,
  estimated nature, access cost, and limits. It is not verified performance
  data and is not part of MVP 1 unless separately approved.

## Historical ideas that are not current decisions

The earlier records proposed a landscape-only pilot, manual diagnostic work,
NZ$197/NZ$500 offers, Google Business Profile work, and consulting services.
They also suggested that a human-reviewed diagnostic may be more commercially
credible than automation.

The current owner direction is different:

- first audience: Auckland small businesses, not a landscaping-only vertical;
- MVP 1: fully automated domain review, teaser, email capture, and free Minimum
  ShortList Assessment;
- MVP 2: test a purchasable Basic Assessment after pricing work;
- MVP 3: consider small-business consulting;
- price remains unknown, although any future displayed price must include GST;
- the product must give value through likely ICPs, evidence-based gaps, a basic
  AI-search component where feasible, and an appropriate upgrade path.

The apparent conflict—historical manual work versus the current fully automated
goal—is a discovery risk to validate, not a reason to silently choose either
model.

## Questions for requirements review

1. What is the smallest generic automated assessment that is valuable across
   Auckland small-business categories without inventing evidence?
2. What exact basic AI-search evidence can be obtained reliably enough for a
   free assessment, and what wording is safe if it cannot be obtained?
3. What automated outcome is acceptable for unreadable, sparse, blocked, or
   ambiguous websites when no manual review is allowed?
4. What must be measured in MVP 1 to show that visitors received value and
   trusted the product enough to share an email address?
5. Which claims need a later technical/provider feasibility spike rather than
   appearing in website copy now?
